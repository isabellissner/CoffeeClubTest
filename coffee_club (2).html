A Field Guide to Processing

How a coffee is processed — the removal of the cherry's fruit layers before drying — is one of the most decisive influences on what ends up in your cup. Here are all the methods in use around the world today.

NaturalDry process · Sun-dried

Natural

The oldest method. Whole cherries are dried in the sun — on patios or raised beds — for 3–6 weeks, intact with skin, pulp and all. Sugars from the fruit slowly penetrate the bean during drying. Requires a dry climate and careful raking to avoid mould.

Blueberry, strawberry, tropical fruit, wine, chocolate, low acidity
Ethiopia · Brazil · Yemen4 of your coffees
WashedWet process

Washed

The most widely used method worldwide. Cherry skin and pulp are removed mechanically within hours of harvest, then beans ferment in water for 12–72 hours to dissolve remaining mucilage, are rinsed clean, and dried on raised beds. The terroir of the bean itself, unobscured by fruit.

Bright acidity, citrus, floral, clean finish, tea-like, light body
Ethiopia · Colombia · Kenya · Central America3 of your coffees
HoneyPulped natural · Miel

Honey

A spectrum between washed and natural. The skin is removed but varying amounts of sticky mucilage (the "honey") are left on the bean during drying. Graded by colour — Yellow → Red → Black — each darker level means more mucilage retained, longer drying, and more fruit character.

Caramel, stone fruit, syrupy sweetness, medium body, balanced acidity
Costa Rica · El Salvador · Brazil
Wet-HulledGiling Basah · Semi-washed

Wet-Hulled

Unique to Indonesia. The cherry is depulped while still very moist, then partially dried before the parchment layer is hulled off while the bean is still wet — the opposite sequence to washed processing. The exposed bean continues drying in humid air, producing a distinctively earthy cup.

Earthy, tobacco, cedar, dark chocolate, heavy body, low acidity
Sumatra · Sulawesi · Java
AnaerobicOxygen-free fermentation

Anaerobic

Cherries or depulped beans are sealed in airtight tanks fitted with CO₂ valves. The absence of oxygen shifts fermentation chemistry dramatically, creating new flavour compounds. After 48–120+ hours the coffee finishes as washed or natural. Can layer on top of any base method.

Intense tropical fruit, rum-like, spice, deep wine complexity
Colombia · Costa Rica · Ethiopia · Global
Carbonic Mac.CM · Wine-inspired

Carbonic Maceration

Borrowed from winemaking. Whole intact cherries are placed in tanks flooded with CO₂, triggering intracellular fermentation inside the fruit itself. Popularised in specialty coffee by 2015 World Barista Champion Sasa Sestic. Produces some of the most complex, wine-like coffees in existence.

Red wine, dark cherry, lavender, intense aromatics, velvety texture
Global — pioneered in Australia, Colombia, Ethiopia
Double FermentedKenya AA · Double-soak

Double Fermented

A washed variant that defines Kenyan specialty coffee. After the first fermentation and wash, beans are soaked again in fresh water for up to 72 hours. This second soak intensifies acidity and produces the electric brightness Kenyan coffees are famous for. Rarely used outside Kenya.

Blackcurrant, tomato-like acidity, intensely juicy, bright
Kenya (almost exclusively)
DecafEA · Swiss Water · Mountain Water · CO₂

Decaffeination

Applied to green beans after harvest processing. EA (Sugarcane) — ethyl acetate from fermented sugarcane; preserves flavour best. Swiss Water — chemical-free water process; 99.9% caffeine removal. Mountain Water — glacial water from Mexico; similar to Swiss. CO₂ — supercritical extraction; expensive but excellent quality retention.

Flavour depends on base process — EA best preserves original character
Colombia (EA) · Canada (Swiss) · Mexico (Mountain)1 of your coffees
ExperimentalLactic · Yeast · Co-fermentation

Experimental

Lactic fermentation cultivates lactic acid bacteria in low-oxygen, low-water conditions for a yoghurt-like creaminess. Yeast inoculation adds selected yeast strains to guide flavour development. Co-fermentation adds fruit juices or spices (pineapple, cinnamon, etc.) to the tank. Divisive but increasingly present at Cup of Excellence competitions.

Highly variable — creamy, spiced, exotic; can be polarising
Colombia · Ethiopia · Panama · Global

Methods are not mutually exclusive — anaerobic fermentation can precede a washed or natural finish. Honey, decaf, and experimental techniques layer on top of base processes. New methods continue to emerge as producers push fermentation science further.
